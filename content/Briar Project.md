
## What is it?

The Briar Project is a nonprofit organization dedicated to developing secure, private, and censorship-resistant communication tools.

Its primary project is **[[Briar]]**, an open-source messaging application designed to continue working even when Internet access is unreliable, censored, or unavailable.

The organization's mission is to give people secure ways to communicate without relying on centralized infrastructure or collecting personal data.

## Legal structure

The Briar Project is a **registered nonprofit organization based in the United Kingdom**.

It exists to develop and steward open-source privacy software rather than generate profits for shareholders.

## Leadership

The Briar Project is led by its core development team, with **Michael Rogers** serving as the project's founder and one of its principal developers.

Development is supported by contributors from the open-source community.

## Relationship to Briar

The Briar Project is the primary steward of the Briar ecosystem.

Its responsibilities include:

- Developing the Briar application
    
- Maintaining the Briar protocol
    
- Publishing open-source software
    
- Coordinating community contributions
    
- Improving security and usability
    
- Promoting secure communication and digital rights

Unlike services such as Signal or Session, the organization does **not** operate centralized messaging servers because Briar communicates directly between users over Tor or local networks.

## Funding

The Briar Project is supported through a combination of:

- Individual donations
    
- Grants
    
- Community support
    
- Volunteer contributions

It does not fund development through advertising or by selling user data.

## Business model

The Briar Project is not a profit-seeking organization.

Briar is free to use, and the project does not monetize users through:

- Advertisements
    
- Selling personal data
    
- Subscription messaging features

Instead, development depends primarily on grants, donations, and community support.

## Main limitations

- The organization is relatively small compared to larger nonprofit technology organizations.
    
- Development depends heavily on grants, donations, and volunteer contributions.
    
- Much of the project's direction is still driven by a small core development team.
    
- Briar currently supports Android only, limiting adoption across devices.

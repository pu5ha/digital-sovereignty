## What is it?

Protocol Labs is an **innovation network** that supports the development of frontier technologies.

It began in 2014 as the organization behind projects such as:

- [[IPFS]]
    
- [[Filecoin]]
    
- [[libp2p]]
    
- [[IPLD]]
    
- [[Multiformats]]

Today, it includes startups, open-source projects, foundations, research groups, investors, accelerators, and service providers.

## Founder

Protocol Labs was founded in **2014** by [[Juan Benet]].

Benet created IPFS as a new way to store and distribute information using content addressing instead of relying only on centralized servers and URLs.

He later proposed Filecoin as an economic network for decentralized storage.

## What does Protocol Labs look like today?

Protocol Labs is no longer best understood as a single company working on a small group of protocols.

It is now a broader network of mostly independent organizations.

```text
Protocol Labs Innovation Network
        │
        ├── Startups
        ├── Open-source projects
        ├── Foundations
        ├── Research teams
        ├── Investment funds
        └── Accelerators
```

Protocol Labs helps connect these organizations through funding, research support, talent, events, operational services, and collaboration.

## Current focus areas

Protocol Labs originally focused on decentralized storage and peer-to-peer infrastructure.

Its current areas of interest include:

- Web3
    
- Artificial intelligence
    
- Robotics
    
- Neurotechnology
    
- Hardware
    
- Digital rights
    
- Open-source infrastructure

## Major projects

|Project|Purpose|
|---|---|
|[[IPFS]]|Peer-to-peer, content-addressed file distribution|
|[[Filecoin]]|Incentivized decentralized storage|
|[[libp2p]]|Modular peer-to-peer networking framework|
|[[IPLD]]|Data model for linking content-addressed information|
|[[Multiformats]]|Self-describing formats for hashes, addresses, and protocols|

These projects remain closely associated with Protocol Labs but are now only one part of the wider network.

## Research

Protocol Labs previously operated a dedicated group called **Protocol Labs Research**.

It worked on areas such as:

- Distributed systems
    
- Cryptography
    
- Networking
    
- Consensus
    
- Public-goods funding

Protocol Labs Research stopped operating as a standalone organization in **2024**. Its researchers and projects continued through other teams across the wider network.

## PL R&D

**PL R&D** supports early-stage research, invention, and prototype development.

Its goal is to help turn promising technical ideas into new projects or companies.

## PL Capital

**PL Capital** invests in companies and technologies that could become venture-scale businesses.

Together, PL R&D and PL Capital support projects from early research through commercialization.

## Open-source philosophy

Open-source development remains central to Protocol Labs.

Many technologies created within the network are:

- Open source
    
- Publicly documented
    
- Permissionless to implement
    
- Designed for interoperability

However, not every company in the wider network is fully open source.

## Funding model

Protocol Labs is not a nonprofit foundation.

Its ecosystem combines:

- Research funding
    
- Venture investment
    
- Grants
    
- Ecosystem funding
    
- Startup investment

Individual organizations within the network may also have their own investors, revenue models, grants, or token-based funding.

## Why it matters

Protocol Labs created several important technologies for the decentralized web.

- IPFS popularized content-addressed file distribution.
    
- libp2p became a widely used peer-to-peer networking framework.
    
- Filecoin created a market for decentralized storage.
    
- IPLD introduced shared models for linking verifiable data.

It has also experimented with a network model in which projects become independent organizations while remaining connected through shared resources and collaboration.

## Main limitations

- The name “Protocol Labs” can refer to the original company, central network teams, or the entire innovation network.
    
- Its network structure can make governance and responsibility difficult to understand.
    
- Protocol Labs remains highly influential even when projects are formally independent.
    
- Its current scope is very broad.
    
- Different organizations in the network may have very different missions, governance structures, and levels of openness.

## What is it?

Nord Security is a cybersecurity company that develops consumer and business security products focused on privacy, identity protection, password management, encrypted storage, and secure networking.

The company is best known for developing:

- [[NordVPN]]
- NordPass
- NordLocker
- NordLayer
- Saily (eSIM service)

Today, Nord Security is one of the world's largest cybersecurity companies.

## Who maintains it?

Nord Security is a privately held company headquartered in **Vilnius, Lithuania**.

The company was founded in 2012 by:

- **Tomas Okmanas**
- **Eimantas Sabaliauskas**

## Leadership

- **Tomas Okmanas** — Co-Founder
- **Eimantas Sabaliauskas** — Co-Founder
- **Jonas Karklys** — Chief Executive Officer

## Team

Approximately **2,000+ employees** across multiple countries.

The company has offices throughout Europe and serves millions of users worldwide.

## Funding

Nord Security was bootstrapped for nearly a decade before raising outside capital.

Notable funding includes:

- **2022:** Raised **$100 million** at a **$1.6 billion valuation**.
- **2023:** Merged with Surfshark, creating one of the world's largest cybersecurity companies while both brands continued to operate independently.

## Business model

Nord Security generates revenue through subscription-based products.

Its primary products include:

- [[NordVPN]]
- NordPass
- NordLocker
- NordLayer
- Saily

The company does not rely on advertising or selling customer data.

## Philosophy

Nord Security aims to make cybersecurity accessible to consumers and businesses by providing tools that improve privacy, security, and digital resilience.

Its products emphasize:

- Strong encryption
- User-friendly design
- High performance
- Independent security audits
- Subscription-funded services
- Privacy protection

## Open-source contributions

Nord Security contributes to several open-source projects and security research initiatives.

Notable examples include:

- The Linux implementation of WireGuard improvements through **NordLynx**
- Bug bounty programs
- Security research
- Open-source libraries and developer tools

However, unlike companies such as Proton, Mullvad, or IVPN, most of Nord Security's consumer applications are **not** open source.

## Main limitations

- Most client applications are proprietary.
- The company focuses on commercial cybersecurity products rather than building public digital infrastructure.
- Following its merger with Surfshark, it became a much larger commercial organization, which some privacy advocates view as increasing the complexity of its corporate structure.

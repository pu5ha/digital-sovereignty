## What is it?

Proton AG is a Swiss technology company that develops privacy-focused online services. Its mission is to build an Internet where individuals, rather than technology companies, control their personal data.

The company is best known for developing:

- Proton Mail
- Proton VPN
- Proton Drive
- Proton Pass
- Proton Calendar
- Proton Wallet

Today, Proton is one of the largest privacy-focused software companies in the world.

## Who maintains it?

Proton AG is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.

The company was founded in 2014 by a group of scientists and engineers who met at CERN, including:

- Dr. Andy Yen (CEO)
- Dr. Jason Stockman
- Dr. Wei Sun

## Leadership

- **Andy Yen** — Founder & Chief Executive Officer
- **Bart Butler** — Chief Technology Officer
- **Christelle Renaud** — Chief Financial Officer

## Team

Approximately **500+ employees** across multiple countries.

The company has grown significantly from its original CERN research team into one of the largest privacy-focused software organizations.

## Funding

Proton was initially funded through a successful crowdfunding campaign and venture funding.

Today, the company is primarily funded through subscriptions to its paid products.

Unlike many technology companies, Proton does **not** rely on advertising or selling user data.

## Business model

Proton operates a subscription-based software business.

Revenue comes from premium subscriptions for:

- Proton Mail
- Proton VPN
- Proton Drive
- Proton Pass
- Proton Unlimited

The company also offers free versions of many products to increase access to privacy tools.

## Open-source contributions

Proton develops and maintains numerous open-source projects, including:

- Proton Mail apps
- Proton VPN apps
- Proton Drive apps
- Proton Pass apps
- Cryptographic libraries
- Privacy and security tooling

The company also funds and contributes to broader open-source and digital rights initiatives.

## Main limitations

- Proton remains a centralized company that operates its own infrastructure.
- Some server-side components are proprietary, even though client applications are largely open source.
- While Switzerland has strong privacy laws, Proton must still comply with lawful Swiss court orders.
